🙌 Your Mission

We are refactoring the warehouse for agents and cutting its cost.

Analysts, operators, executives, and agents depend on the warehouse. The same question has to return one answer.

As Senior Analytics Engineer, you own the transformation layer in dbt and Snowflake. You make it dependable: well modelled, tested, documented, and clear enough that a person or an agent can trust an answer. You are the data-engineering bar for the team, and you work alongside our other analytics engineer.

You get up to speed on the stack we have, then help build the post-AI data stack: artifacts an agent can read, tools an agent can operate, context we own, and one reality the company can check.

You set the quality standard by example. You are strong enough to shape the data stack over the long run.

🎯 Your Responsibilities In This Role

- Own the modelling and transformation of your domains end to end in dbt and Snowflake, from the business question through to the governed model that answers it

- Set and defend modelling methodology alongside our existing Analytics Engineer, grain, dimensional patterns, and where the exceptions genuinely earn their place

- Make your models agent-ready: semantically rich, consistently named, described in business terms, and safe for something to query without a person checking the output

- Own streaming and near-real-time pipelines (Kafka or similar) alongside batch transformation, keeping real-time flows reliable and cost-efficient before they become a bottleneck

- Establish and enforce coding and agentic-coding standards, systematic testing and documentation as CI-enforced defaults

- Optimise warehouse performance and cost, finding and fixing the query patterns and materialisation choices driving unnecessary spend

- Deploy and supervise autonomous agents against our data stack, and hold our data infrastructure to proper engineering standards: monitored, wired to incident.io and PagerDuty, with escalations, SLAs and documented processes

- Support and mentor analysts on analytics-engineering practice, raising the engineering standard across the team

🔥 Must-have requirements

- Three or more years of production dbt. You can defend a modelling methodology. Kimball sits inside that defence: grain, facts, dimensions, and where a wide table is the wrong choice

- A semantic layer you have used in production. You can explain metrics, dimensions, grain, and joins, and how an agent reads them

- Streaming and warehouse scale. You have owned CDC or another stream, incremental transforms, and a freshness choice with a cost. You can say when a batch model is the wrong place for a high-frequency number, and what sits beside the warehouse: a stream, a low-latency table, or a semantic API

- Snowflake or Databricks. You have worked on cost and scale: incremental versus full refresh, clustering, and a lag target a consumer needs

- Orchestration. You have scheduled, monitored, and repaired a pipeline in Airflow, Dagster, or an equivalent

- CI/CD and deployment management. You have run a project where a pull request builds the changed models and their downstream dependencies against production state, writes them to an isolated schema or clone, and promotes that same selection after merge. You can describe the path from a developer environment through CI into production, how a bad deploy is stopped or rolled back, and the signal that a deploy succeeded

- Three or more years of production Python. You have shipped Python in a pipeline: transforms and checks that run without you. A take-home test, or a notebook, is not this requirement

- You are passionate about building with agents and for agents. Serving agents data at scale is a problem you want. You have used frontier tooling to automate a traditional analytics workload, such as a recurring model change, a reconciliation, or a report. You can show the outcome in time or volume. You can explain how that system is observed, how it is governed, and how it scales past one person on a laptop

💪 Nice to have requirements

- An ontology, or a business entity model, on top of a semantic layer

- A distributed engine such as Spark or Trino, used on data that did not fit a single warehouse query

- Fintech, payments, or another regulated environment

➡️ Interview Process

Before you apply, take a look at our Extra Preparation guide, covering our engineering philosophy, tech stack, and what to expect at each interview stage.

You will need to first submit your application through our ATS Workable. There is no need to submit a Cover Letter.

If successful you will be selected for our interview process, which has the following stages:

- [30mins] Interview with the Talent Team

- We want to get to know you and follow up on your experience and motivations. The best preparation is to really know why you applied for the role, including your application questions.

- [45mins] Interview with the Hiring Manager

- You'll meet Parth, who leads our Data team. This goes deep on how you have used dbt to solve business problems, how you think about modelling, and how you actually work with AI. The best preparation is to know what's on your CV really well.

- Take-Home Task

- A real-world task, scoped at around 2 to 3 hours, with a week to return it. Full use of AI tooling is encouraged, and we ask you to share your prompts alongside the work. It is how we build, and we would rather see it than pretend otherwise

- [1hr] Technical Deep Dive with the Data Team

- You present your take-home to two members of the data team and field questions on your architecture and decisions.

- [45mins] Bar Raiser

- A final conversation with a member of our leadership team about track record, ambition and motivations, and your chance to ask whatever you still want to know about the business
