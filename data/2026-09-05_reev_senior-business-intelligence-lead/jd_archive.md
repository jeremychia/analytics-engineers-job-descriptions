Your Mission

EV charging is evolving from a niche technology into everyday infrastructure. As reev grows, teams increasingly need reliable, consistently defined metrics. Today, different dashboards, reporting dates, filters, and assumptions can produce different answers to the same business question.
This role addresses the problem - not by building more dashboards, but by creating a shared and trustworthy data foundation. The goal is to establish clear definitions and consistent metrics so that teams and leadership can make informed decisions based on the same numbers. Someone who understands that unclear definitions, rather than a lack of data, are often the real problem can make a significant impact in this role. You own the certified layer, the single, agreed upon version of every core metric that reev runs on. You connect the definition, the data model, and the delivery. And when two numbers disagree, you are the person who says which one to trust.

What you will do

Define core metrics with Finance, Product, Sales, Marketing, Customer Success, Engineering, and Platform.

Agree on definitions, data contracts, and ownership for core metrics with the teams that own them.

Maintain and verify the certified data layer, built in ETL tool and surfaced in Metabase, so teams can build their own dashboards on top of it.

Use SQL to validate data, investigate inconsistencies, and trace numbers back to their sources.

Work with Platform team to identify potential improvements in data flows, pipelines, and transformations, advise on next steps, and help prioritize next steps during planning.

Help stakeholders decide which number to trust when reports disagree.

Improve data literacy through practical guidance, workshops, and clear documentation.

Use AI-assisted tools for analysis, documentation, automation, and workflows. Check the results carefully.

What this role is not

You will not be the central owner of every dashboard request. Teams remain responsible for their own dashboards, analyses, and data models built on their data contracts. This is also not a classic infrastructure or people-management role.
