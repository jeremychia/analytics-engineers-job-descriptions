About the Company
Nordic Tyre Group is the largest tyre wholesaler in the Nordic countries and the Baltics. We use the power of technology to connect leading retailers and their customers with the world's best tyres. We offer products for passenger cars, trucks, construction and agricultural machinery. Learn more about the company at nordictyregroup.com.
We are currently present in six markets: Norway, Sweden, Finland, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania. The group has about 500 employees and a turnover of 4,3 mdkr.
Join our team of great talents!
Why this role exists
Nordic Tyre Group is transforming a decades-long, successful tyre wholesaling business into a modern, data-driven one. We believe our future is in data, and we have ambitious growth goals that depend on it.
Over the past two years we have standardised finance across all business units on Microsoft Business Central, rebuilt our B2B portal, and put demand forecasting, churn, cross-sell and segmentation models into the business. The foundations are in place. What we need now is someone to turn that foundation into a coherent analytics capability — and to make the rest of the group fluent in it.
The role

We are looking for a curious-minded Analytics Engineer to build, maintain and optimise our analytics stack, which runs on Microsoft Fabric and is built on dbt.
The hands-on engineering is half the job. The other half is the analytics framework itself: the metric definitions, modelling standards and ways of working that let the group ask better questions and trust the answers. This is a role for someone who is as interested in what the numbers mean as in how they are produced.
What you will do

Analytics engineering
– Build and maintain modular, well-tested dbt models on Microsoft Fabric.
– Optimise existing transformations for performance, cost and maintainability.
– Own data models end to end — from source through to the semantic layer.
Build the analytics framework
– Define and document the metrics that matter across the group, so that one metric has one definition everywhere.
– Set modelling standards, naming conventions and testing practice — and hold the line on them.
– Build reusable structures so that new questions do not each require a new pipeline.
Turn data into decisions
– Develop Power BI datasets and reports that stakeholders genuinely use.
– Go past reporting into analysis: identify what the numbers mean and what should change as a result.
– Work directly with commercial, supply chain and finance teams to frame the questions worth answering.
Bring people with you
– Explain analytical work to non-technical stakeholders clearly and without condescension.
– Raise data literacy across the group and make self-service genuinely usable rather than nominally available.
Protect the foundation
– Build testing, monitoring and documentation into the pipeline as standard.
– Treat data quality as a feature of the product, not a cleanup task.

Beyond the tooling

Two qualities matter more to us than any single technology on the list above.
– Analytics. A genuine instinct for what a number is telling you, what it is hiding, and which question to ask next.
– Pedagogy. The ability to teach — to make an analytical result land with a stakeholder who has no interest in how it was built.
Alongside those: curiosity, comfort with the ambiguity of a business mid-transformation, and the judgement to know when a pragmatic answer today beats a perfect one next month.
