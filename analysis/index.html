AE/BI job market: what job ads ask, and what they leave out

Why read job ads?

Ads show what a field expects of its people

One study read millions of newspaper job ads from 1940 to 2000. The job titles stayed the same, and the work inside them changed. These ads are a first snapshot of analytics engineering, to compare with later ones.

They come from one person's job search, mostly in English, so they lean senior. Read every number as this snapshot's habit, not the size of the market. Every chart below is worked out live, so it follows the region filter at the top. Four questions, four answers:

1 · What is an analytics engineer?

The ads don't agree where the job stops. They do share a core.

Two ads with the same title can describe different jobs. Underneath, most ask for the same few things, and the tools an ad names say which job it really is. This chapter goes from the title, to the duties, to the tools, to where your own tools land.

2 · Do ads keep up with the work?

Ads describe the team we have, not the one we are building

An ad is written from the team that exists today, often from the last person hired. New work turns up in ads late, and often under an older name. AI is the clearest case: who asks for it, what they mean, and whether the ask is growing.

3 · What do ads leave unsaid?

The ad asks a lot and tells little

The employer knows the pay, the team, the interview and what it takes to move up. The candidate knows what the ad says. The first half of this chapter is about the job on offer. The second is about the path up, which ads leave unwritten too.

4 · Who will be senior next?

Few ways in today means few seniors tomorrow

This is the finding that matters most for the field. Chapter 3 showed that mentoring climbs with seniority: senior ads ask people to teach. But today's juniors are the seniors teams will need in five years. If few of them get in, who will today's seniors mentor?

Where that leaves us

No one knows exactly. The job description is still ours to write.

There is no shared template for the job, so each ad is a choice. Write down what you expect, what it pays, what it takes to move up, and who will teach.

If you're hiringStart from the shared core, then name what is yours. State the pay, the interview and who will teach the new hire.
If you're moving upPractise the next verb on the work you already do, and move closer to the business. If you mentor, say so in your team's junior ad.
If you're starting outThe core is the same everywhere, and no two ads are alike. Apply to the doors that exist, and pitch your own mix.
