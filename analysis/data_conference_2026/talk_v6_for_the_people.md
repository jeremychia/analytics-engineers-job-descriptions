# Let them in, help them up, teach them — v6, for a senior room

Draft 3, 6 November 2026. The deck is `jd_deck_v6.html` (six slides and one for questions, built from `build/src/slides_v6.js`). It reorders v5 (`talk_v5_for_the_people.md`, `jd_deck_v5.html`) for a 5-minute slot in front of a senior room.

## The shape

- **The opening:** senior ads that ask for AI name a semantic layer nearly twice as often, 46% against 25%. It is about the room's own ads, it is the question they are already asking, and it holds at the same level and ad length (p < 0.001).
- **The bridge:** AI needs the business written down. Who teaches that?
- **The argument:** the ads ask for the same skills at every level. What changes is the verb, and at senior the verb is mentor. Yet three in four junior ads don't say who will teach, and the junior ad asks for a degree more than any other level.
- **The one action:** if you mentor, say so in the junior ad.
- **Tested, and said so aloud:** the opening gap, the verb steps, and the degree gap. The "who teaches" count is a hand read of every junior ad, so it is a count, not a test.
- **Spoken numbers:** 1,500, 46 against 25, four, 95%, a third, a third of senior ads, 85, 20, 65, half, more than half, five, 2–5 years.
- **Tone:** the ads are the subject, never the people who write them.

## The script (about 3:18 at 130 words a minute, about 430 words; about 3:40 with the pauses)

**[First · What does AI ask of seniors?]**
"Most of you have been asked what AI means for your team. I read about 1,500 job ads for analytics engineers to see what they say. Here is what the senior ones say.

Senior ads that ask for AI name a semantic layer nearly twice as often: 46 percent against 25. That holds when you compare ads at the same level and of the same length. It is not chance.

One ad says why. Multiverse is rebuilding its semantic layer so people and AI agents can query trusted metrics directly.

Context engineering is the phrase at every conference this year. Four ads use it. The work is already there, under an older name: the business, written down so a machine can read it.

AI needs the business written down. Who teaches that?"

**[Help them up · What moves you up?]**
"You'd expect the ads to ask for more of the business as you go up. They don't.

SQL is in ninety-five percent of ads, at every level. Understanding the business is named in about a third, also at every level. Tristan Handy says the path up is for people curious how the business works. The ads don't show it.

Same skills. So what moves you up?"

**[Help them up · What changes at each step?]**
"In the ads, the answer is the verb. 'Support' falls and 'own' rises.

And from mid to senior, one verb arrives: mentor. A third of senior ads ask you to mentor, coach or review code. Each of those steps holds beyond chance."

**[Pass it on · Who teaches the juniors?]**
"Raise your hand if you mentor someone. Keep it up if your last junior ad said so.

That gap is in the ads too. I read all eighty-five junior ads by hand. Twenty say who will teach the new hire. The other sixty-five don't.

Half name neither a teacher nor anything to learn with. The best one I found is from a bank in New Zealand: time to train, and seniors to learn from.

The senior who would teach them is already on the team. They're just not in the ad."

**[Let them in · What stands in the way?]**
"And the way in is narrower than it needs to be. More than half of junior ads mention a degree. At every other level, it's a third. That gap is not chance either. Only five ads in 1,500 name another way in."

**[The ad again]**
"One last ad. Enhance is hiring a junior analytics engineer, and it asks for two to five years. It got one line right: 'Strong internships or graduate projects count.' Keep that. Cut the years. And add the line most junior ads are missing: who will teach them.

If you mentor, say so in the junior ad."

**Retired from the script after review:**
- "Ten seniors asked to mentor for every junior told who." It set a sample estimate against a full count with a different rule, and most of the ratio came from the 9-to-1 mix.
- "Junior ads say 'support', mid-level ads say 'own'." Mid-level ads still say "support" more often than "own".
- "The more senior the ad, the more it asks for AI." Ad length explains most of the climb above junior.
- "Junior ads need the local language more often." 20% against 10% across all ads, but 8% against 6% on English ads alone (not significant). The gap comes from which languages the junior ads are in.
- "Two in three AI-tool asks never mention checking the output." True only within the same sentence (35% mention it). Counting the sentence either side, 58% do.
- "Junior ads state pay least often." Outside the US it is flat (junior 15%, mid 15%, senior 15%, lead 23%). The gap comes from how few junior ads are American.
- "Junior ads ask for AI tools as often as senior ads." True on English ads (36% against 37%, only 53 junior ads), not across all languages (25% against 34%).
- The pay lines. "A quarter give the pay" is driven by US ads. The EU directive does not require pay in the ad, and the reviewer reported that France had not yet written it into law.

## Q&A ready

- **"How many junior ads are there at all?"** For every junior ad there are eight at senior level or above. The ads were collected during one job search, which leans senior, so the talk doesn't lead with it.
- **"How many junior ads ask for years?"** One in six asks for two years or more. ITT says junior in its title and asks for two. Dataroots has junior in its web address and asks for three.
- **"Isn't one in eight just what you collected?"** Partly. The ads were collected during one person's job search, which leans senior. That is why the script says "in the ads I read".
- **"Do juniors come in as analysts instead?"** Possibly. The corpus is analytics-engineering and BI ads. Analyst roles are a likely door the corpus does not cover.
- **"Doesn't a learning budget count as support?"** It does, and it's counted: 26 junior ads offer a budget, a programme, courses or set time to learn. Most of those still don't say who will teach. Half of junior ads offer neither.
- **"An ad that says nothing doesn't mean nobody mentors."** Agreed. The point is what a junior can see before applying. The mentoring may exist; the ad doesn't say so.
- **"Do ads that name a mentor get better hires?"** The ads can't show outcomes. Ask the room.
- **"Isn't AI why there are fewer junior roles?"** The ads can't show a trend: a few months of ads, collected during one search. Stanford's US data is the best outside signal, and its authors say it is descriptive. What the ads show is the job moving from writing to reviewing.
- **"What about AI and the junior job?"** A few ads show the first job changing. Zego's says "AI tools now handle a lot of the heavy lifting of writing SQL", and the new hire reviews it. 31 junior ads ask for AI, and 7 of them say who will teach. Junior ads with no AI ask are about the same (13 of 54), so it is a count, not a gap. Outside these ads, Stanford finds US employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs 19% below trend; the authors call it a pattern, not proof.
- **"Isn't the semantic layer just a Power BI thing?"** Power BI calls its datasets "semantic models", and 86 ads use that phrase. The rest name dbt's semantic layer, MetricFlow, a metrics layer or a semantic layer in general. Multiverse, KnowBe4 and Finom tie theirs to AI agents directly.
- **"Isn't the AI ad just a longer, more senior ad?"** That was the first check. Ads that ask for AI are longer and more senior on average, so the gap was tested within each level and each third of ad length. It holds.
- **"Isn't a degree a fair proxy when there's no experience?"** Maybe. But only 15 of the 46 say "or equivalent experience", or words like it, anywhere in the ad. Saying that projects count shows the other way in.
- **"So are ads dishonest about what gets you promoted?"** I can't say. Ads are written from templates and from the last hire, and they describe the job, not the promotion. The ads can't show what actually gets people promoted. Ask the room.
- **"Isn't business knowledge hard to put in an ad?"** Yes, and that may be the point. SQL is easy to list, so it gets listed. The things that move people up are harder to write down.
- **"Will we all be context engineers?"** At the dbt summit, Joe Reis heard the job described as moving from analytics engineer to context engineer. Handy thinks it unlikely everyone gets retitled. In the ads, 4 of 1,478 say "context engineering", and 22 titles mention AI. The work is there under its older name, the semantic layer.
- **"Why don't the ads just say it?"** Handy said something on the same podcast: in data, the hard part is often not what we don't know, but what we know and don't want to face. Everyone here knows a junior needs someone to learn from. The ad is where that becomes a promise.
- **"Won't the titles change anyway?"** The Seattle Data Guy expects titles like "AI engineer" or "Forward Deployed Engineer". In these ads it has barely started: 22 of 1,478 titles mention AI, mostly as "AI & Analytics Engineer", and 2 say "forward deployed". The search was for analytics-engineering titles, so renamed roles may be missing.
- **"Won't the stack consolidate?"** He expects it to. Then "the rest is local" gets shorter, and SQL and people stay the floor.
- **"Will AI change the job faster than the ads?"** Handy expects the technical parts of data work to change the way software engineering did, and fast. He expects the part that stays to be knowing the business. That is the line on "Same asks" that the ads name in only a third, at every level.
- **"Aren't some people just attached to writing SQL?"** Reis said as much: some people are attached to the identity of "I can write really complex SQL". SQL is in 95% of ads, so it is the floor, not the step.
- **"Isn't this about headcount, not wording?"** Headcount is often not ours to decide. The mentor line in the ad is.
- **"What should my ad offer?" or "What can I ask for?"** Go to slide 10. Each region has its own norm, as a % of ads that list each benefit. A learning budget is listed in 14% of Nordic ads and 38% of CEE ads. Read the ads around you as the norm.
- **Pay transparency:**
  - **Outside the US:** about 15% of ads state pay.
  - **The EU directive:** it gives applicants the right to the pay range before the interview, not necessarily in the ad.
  - **France:** check its transposition status before the talk.
- **Stack:** at mid level, dbt ads without Power BI ask people to define, own or lead 22% of the time; Power BI ads without dbt, 4%. A different stack offers a different room to grow.
