# Rethink: what 1,200 job ads say about how this field grows people

Draft, 27 October 2026. Adopted as v4 (`talk_v4_for_the_people.md`, `jd_deck_v4.html`). The hand reads that followed changed three numbers below; they are corrected here. Numbers marked *first pass* come from text patterns checked by reading their matches, not from a blind hand-read.

## The new question

v3 reads ads as a candidate. v4 reads them as the person who writes one. Both treat the ad as the thing to fix.

The new paradigm treats the ads as the field's public record of how it lets people in, helps them up, and asks them to lift others. The question for the room becomes: **what does that record say, and what can each of us do to help the next person?**

That speaks to everyone in a senior room:
- **Seniors:** as mentors.
- **Managers:** as people who open the door.
- **Juniors:** as people the field should be making room for.

## Your question: do seniors face the same gates?

They face more of them. The junior "gate" rule counts things that are normal at other levels.

| Share of ads that… | Junior (64) | Mid (573) | Senior (470) | Lead+ (104) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ask for 2+ years of experience | 19% | 46% | 60% | 55% |
| need a language other than English | 17% | 13% | 9% | 6% |
| mention a visa or clearance | 3% | 10% | 10% | 11% |
| mention a degree | 58% | 30% | 33% | 33% |
| say projects or equivalent experience count | 16% | 9% | 10% | 11% |

**What we should now say:**
- **Drop "four in ten junior ads carry a gate".** It bundles asks that are ordinary at senior level with asks that contradict the junior label.
- **What is specific to juniors:** one junior ad in five asks for two years or more. Junior ads mention a degree almost twice as often as senior ads. They also need the local language more often (17% against 9%).
- **Visa is not a junior problem** in these ads: 3% of junior ads mention one, against 10% at other levels.
- **What helps instead:** one junior ad in six says projects, a portfolio or equivalent experience count.

**What the ads cannot tell you:**
- whether juniors who got in were mentored;
- how long promotion really takes;
- whether people from other backgrounds get hired;
- what good mentoring looks like.

Say this plainly, then ask the room. It turns Q&A into a conversation.

## A new spine: let them in, help them up, pass it on

**Opening (0:40): the Enhance ad.** "Junior. Two to five years. Projects count." Ask: if this were your first ad, how would you get in?

**1. Let them in (1:15)**
- **Slides:** the pipeline (1 junior ad to 9 senior), then a new chart of the gates by level. The two junior-specific rows are highlighted: years against the label, and the degree.
- **Action, for managers:** write "projects count", and ask only for the years you need.
- **Action, for juniors:** the ads with no such ask exist. Apply, and lead with the project.

**2. Help them up (1:15)**
- **Slide:** the ladder, with the core flat and the verb climbing.
- **Advice for mentors, from the data:**
  - **Mid to senior:** let them review code and set one standard.
  - **Senior to lead:** hand them a roadmap.
- **The line:** "Hand them the next verb."

**3. Pass it on (1:00)**
- **Slide:** a new mentoring-gap chart. 39% of senior ads ask you to mentor; about 1 in 6 junior ads says who will.
- **Point:** analytics engineering is already an enabling job. Half of senior and lead ads ask you to make others self-sufficient.
- **Action:** if you mentor, put it in the junior ad. Name the person.

**4. The field (0:40)**
- **Slide:** what ads leave out.
- **Point:** with no shared definition and no template, each team writes the path down for itself, or no one does.
- **Ask of the industry:** publish the ladder, the pay and the mentor.

**Close (0:15):** "Someone held the door for each of us. Hold it for the next one."

**Cut:** AI, benefits, the stack slide and the tool-mix map. Each answers a candidate's question, not "how do we grow people". They stay in the 10-minute deck.

## Before this becomes a script

1. **Mentoring gap:** hand-read all 64 junior ads in full for "names who will guide you", and recount the senior mentoring ask on all languages. It is the headline, so it needs the strongest check.
2. **Enabling-others pattern:** read a sample of 30 matches and 30 non-matches.
3. **Gates by level:** decide whether "degree mentioned" should become "degree required". Mentioned includes "or equivalent".
4. **Outside check:** a job board's count of junior against senior analytics postings for the 1-to-9 ratio.
