joe reis (JR):
- a lot of execs and VCs who want to go back into builder mode
- the number of books are going up whereas the reading rate is going down - there's going to be a higher demand of high quality books
- at the dbt summit: the transition from an analytics engineer to a context engineer
- there are people who are attached to the identity that "i can write really complex SQL.."
- how do you keep people on dashboards these days when they can build things with agent?

tristan handy (TH):
- "i read a lot and i am good at integrating a lot of sources into a world model. and i am good at speaking and writing about that"
- am reading a "ton", and "rebuilding my world model"
- as knowledge workers, we're struggling - "the thing we love to do is what the world has rewarded more than anything else. and now we're asking: is our passion really necessary?"
- "i had a career that paid my salary, and i want to know how to position myself to continue being paid that salary in the future"
- the role of the software engineer has changed the most aggressively - because there is a lot of training data, the context is highly observable, and success is very measurable. the more technical components of data work will move in that direction rapidly
- in analysis, it's not like an "agent factory" because you don't know when the agent is done
- analytics engineering - building structural knowledge of the business, and now will be curating context around it. it seems unlikely that everyone will be retitled "context engineers"
- one of the ways good companies hired good people in the past - you have agency to choose interesting projects you love, but now it seems like we've "come down to earth" -> that some people would like to be insulated from the messiness of "how do we grow the business". -> it seems like now we don't really need people to do that anymore
- the job of the data team historically: catalog of information, and answering business questions. the catalog is getting more efficient. the part that is new is to connect all of the data to AI -> making sure you've built the systems which are able to do that, preparing organisational change (who is responsible for what). 
- data people are hugely influential, and should not stay attached to the way things worked before. "you will get run over the arc of history".
- when you give a task to an agent, you will want to them to give you an output at an appropriate level of abstraction
- we need agent-focused tooling / point-and-click (viz tools) is going to be a back-up.
- i want data observability that says "you're tracking behind this metrics .. here's the hypotheiss for why that could be and these are my recommended next steps"
- blind spots in the industry: "what we are dealing with in data is not the 'unknowns', it is the things that we 'know' but do not want to face". -> it's an organisational problem
- we get stuck in an equilibrium and we get so comfortable that we don't want to adapt.
- the way you deliver value to the business will be flipped on its head.
